In a new essay for The Domination Chronicles, I look back at Walter Echo-Hawk’s *In the Light of Justice* (2013) and the foreword James Anaya wrote for it. Anaya roots the human rights problems Indigenous peoples face in “patterns of domination set in motion by colonization,” yet neither he nor Echo-Hawk names U.S. federal Indian law and policy as a present-day system of domination. Instead, the book turns to atonement, reparations, and “reconciliation,” a word that can mean bringing someone “to acquiescence and quiet submission.” I point out that the book’s index has no listing for “domination,” that Echo-Hawk does not explain Chief Justice Marshall’s word “ascendency” in *Johnson v. M’Intosh*, and that his call to restore “a balance” between dominionism and Indigenous worldviews assumes a balance that never existed. My view is that we need to accurately name the U.S. claim of a right of domination and then challenge it unceasingly.
